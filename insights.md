# What we've learned

- 18 Nov 2026: the first 4 reels (brake failure, shrink, car in a lake, empty plane) were 45 to 55 seconds and opened without on-screen hook text. The owner saw only a few views on each. Changes: 30 to 40 second target, big hook text from frame 0, a "send this to..." card, a question as the first comment, and wider spacing between posts.
- 19 Nov 2026 (stats for the 18 Nov posts; YouTube numbers not in yet, Metricool lag):
  - Brake failure beat everyone-shrank on every network: Instagram 1,274 vs 206 views, 38.3 s vs 17.0 s average watch time, 3 vs 0 saves, 2 vs 0 shares; Facebook 232 vs 4 views. A numbered, practical payoff ("6 steps, how many did you know?") keeps people watching to the end and gets saves. Give every reel a countdown or numbered list of surprising, checkable facts, and ask for a number in the comments.
  - Skip rate in the first 3 seconds was high on both (39% and 37%). Both reels predate the frame-0 hook text, so keep the hook lines short and alarming and check the skip rate on the 19 Nov reels.
  - Pure fantasy without a "you didn't know this" twist lost half its viewers by the middle. For fantasy topics (invisible, shrinking), lead with the twist that flips the fantasy and pay it off with real science.
- 20 Nov 2026 (stats for the 19 Nov posts, plane and car-in-lake; YouTube numbers still not in, Metricool lag):
  - The frame-0 hook text did not lower the 3-second skip rate: plane 45.8% and car-in-lake 59.8%, against 39.4% for brake failure. Average watch time was only 12.5 s and 11.3 s on about 35 s reels, with 0 shares. Brake failure is still the only reel that spreads (1,301 views, 38.0 s average watch, 4 saves, 2 shares). Its first line puts a hard, checkable number on the danger ("every second you travel 28 metres"). Open every survival reel with one concrete number the viewer can feel, in the first spoken sentence and in the hook text.
  - Brake failure walked through 6 numbered steps; car-in-lake squeezed its tip into an acronym and the plane reel into radio jargon. Keep the payoff as plain, numbered, one-action-per-step instructions (show the step number big on screen) rather than acronyms or jargon.
  - Facebook liked the 19 Nov survival reels as much as brake failure (plane 233, car-in-lake 258, brake 233 views), while everyone-shrank got 7. Survival and danger topics are the safe bet on Facebook.
- 21 Nov 2026 (trapped-in-lift run): Metricool's analytics endpoint returned empty rows and then 502 errors twice, so no new numbers this run. Carried the 20 Nov lessons into the reel instead: a concrete number in the first spoken line and hook headline ("0 bars"), four plain numbered steps shown big on screen, and a myth-buster twist (you won't run out of air) as the open loop's payoff.
- 22 Nov 2026 (stats for the 19 to 21 Nov posts; YouTube YTVP06/YTVP08 still return empty rows four days in, so YouTube numbers may not be reaching Metricool):
  - Invisible (20 Nov) had the best watch time of the new reels (16.8 s on Instagram, 16 s on Facebook, 285 Facebook views) and the first share since brake failure. Its hook promised a count with an open loop: "3 things go wrong, and the last one ruins everything". Keep the "N things / the last one" promise in the hook and pay off the last item at the end.
  - Quicksand (21 Nov, cartoon) got the most Instagram views of the new reels (311) but only 13.7 s average watch and 42.6% skip. Fridge (comedy, no facts) had the worst skip rate of the week apart from car-in-lake (46.8%). The reels with the lowest skip rates, brake failure (39.7%) and invisible (40.0%), both open on "you" plus an instant consequence.
  - Watch time is still about 11 to 17 s on 35 s reels for everything except brake failure (37.8 s, 1,310 views, 4 saves, 2 shares). Brake failure's steps are each an action the viewer can picture doing with their hands. Make every step a physical action, and put the step number on screen the moment it is spoken.
