# Sources: What if your phone caught fire while charging?

Checked 20 Nov 2026.

| Claim in the reel | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Warning signs: smoke, extreme heat, swelling, hissing or cracking, unusual smell | Scottish Fire and Rescue Service, "Lithium-ion batteries": "producing smoke", "feeling extremely hot to touch", "looking swollen", "making a hissing or cracking sound", "smelling unusual" (https://www.firescotland.gov.uk/at-home/lithium-ion-batteries). CFA Victoria: "popping, hissing or crackling sounds, smoke and fumes" (https://www.cfa.vic.gov.au/plan-prepare/fires-in-the-home/charging-and-battery-safety) |
| Under a pillow, the heat has nowhere to go | Engadget: "With it tucked under your pillow, the growing heat can't dissipate into the open air like it normally would" (https://engadget.com/2309290/why-charging-phone-under-pillow-dangerous-explained). Fire and Emergency NZ: "do not charge these under a pillow, on the bed or on a couch – they can overheat and cause a fire" (https://www.fireandemergency.nz/lithium-ion-battery-safety/) |
| Thermal runaway: the battery makes heat faster than it can lose it | Engadget: "thermal runaway, where your phone starts generating heat faster than it can get rid of it" |
| It can burst into flames without warning / fire spreads fast | Scottish Fire and Rescue: "it can overheat and catch fire without warning", "they can explode and/or lead to a rapidly developing fire" |
| The smoke is toxic | Fire and Emergency NZ: "Battery gases, vapour and smoke are highly toxic and flammable and must not be inhaled". CFA Victoria: they release "toxic, flammable, explosive gases" |
| Don't touch it / don't try to fight it | CFA Victoria: "Don't touch the device." Scottish Fire and Rescue: "Do not try to extinguish the fire yourself." |
| Get out, close the door, call for help from outside | Fire and Emergency NZ: "Evacuate immediately and close doors (if safe) to slow the spread of fire", "Once in a safe location, call 111". Scottish Fire and Rescue: "get out, stay out, and call 999" |
| In India, call 112 | Single emergency number 112 (ERSS), see reels/car-in-lake/sources.md: Business Standard, "Dial 112 for help in any emergency" (https://www.business-standard.com/article/pti-stories/dial-112-for-help-in-any-emergency-to-kick-off-in-14-states-uts-on-feb-19-119021400942_1.html) |
| Charge on a hard, flat surface, not on a bed | Engadget: "hard, flat and well-ventilated surface". CFA Victoria: "Don't charge batteries or devices on or under soft surfaces (for example, beds, pillows, sofas and blankets)" |
| Don't charge while you sleep | Scottish Fire and Rescue: "you should not charge your battery while you are asleep or away from home". CFA Victoria: "Don't charge your phone when you're sleeping. You may not wake up if it starts a fire." |
| Use the original or approved charger | Scottish Fire and Rescue: "use the manufacturer-approved charger", "avoid counterfeit goods as they pose a serious fire risk". Fire and Emergency NZ: "Only use chargers and batteries supplied with the device, or certified third-party charging equipment" |

Dropped: dropping a small device in a bucket of water (NZ allows it "if this can be done safely", Scotland says not to fight it yourself); the reel keeps the advice every source agrees on.
