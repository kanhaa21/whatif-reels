# Sources: What if you stepped into quicksand? (3D cartoon short)

Checked 21 Nov 2026.

| Claim | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Quicksand is about twice as dense as the human body (about 2 g/ml vs about 1 g/ml), so you can't sink completely; you sink to about the waist | Nature news, 28 Sep 2005, on Daniel Bonn's study (https://www.nature.com/articles/news050926-9); National Geographic, "Quicksand Science: Why It Traps, How to Escape" (https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/quicksand-science-why-it-traps-how-to-escape/); Live Science, "Quicksand Myth Debunked: You Can Float Free" (https://www.livescience.com/9373-quicksand-myth-debunked-float-free.html) |
| Pulling a trapped foot straight out takes force comparable to lifting a car | National Geographic (Bonn: about 10^6 newtons to pull a foot out at 1 cm/s, comparable to lifting a medium-sized car) |
| Moving makes you sink; the sand then packs around you and holds you | Nature news; National Geographic |
| Escape: wiggle the legs slowly to let water in and loosen the sand, lean back to spread your weight, and float/ease out | National Geographic; Live Science; BBC Science Focus (https://www.sciencefocus.com/news/simple-trick-to-escape-quicksand) |
| Films show people swallowed whole; that does not happen | Britannica, "How deadly is quicksand?" (https://www.Britannica.Com/story/how-deadly-is-quicksand) |
