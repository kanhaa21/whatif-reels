# Sources: What if your car broke down in the middle of nowhere?

Checked 22 Nov 2026. Everyday-emergencies queue, topic 3.

| Claim in the reel | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Don't walk along the highway for help; stay with the car and wait | Queensland Government, "Breakdown safety on motorways and freeways": "Don't walk along highways or motorways." and "If you don't have a mobile phone and it isn't safe to walk to a telephone, stay with your car and wait for help." (https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/vehicle-safety/crashes-and-breakdowns/breakdown-safety) |
| Step 1: hazard lights on | India, Motor Vehicles (Driving) Regulations 2017, reg. 28 "Vehicle breakdown": "the hazard warning lights of the vehicle shall be switched on immediately" (https://future.indiankanoon.org/doc/137123717/). UK Highway Code rule 277: "switch your hazard warning lights on" (https://www.gov.uk/guidance/the-highway-code/breakdowns-and-incidents-274-to-287) |
| Step 1: get the car fully off the road | Highway Code rule 276: "get your vehicle off the road if possible". State Farm: "pull over as far onto the shoulder as possible" (https://www.statefarm.com/simple-insights/auto-and-vehicles/what-to-do-if-your-car-breaks-down). Queensland: "pull your vehicle onto the road shoulder, or if no shoulder exists, as far away from traffic as possible" |
| Step 2: get out on the side away from traffic (the left side in India, where traffic keeps left) | Highway Code rule 277: "exit your vehicle by the side furthest from traffic, if it is safe and possible to do so". Queensland: "exit through the passenger side of the vehicle to keep away from the traffic." |
| Step 2: never stand behind your car (between it and oncoming traffic) | Highway Code rule 276: "do not stand (or let anybody else stand) between your vehicle and oncoming traffic"; rule 277: "DO NOT stand in a place where your vehicle could be forced into you if moving traffic collides with it". Rule 275: "keep well away from your vehicle and moving traffic." |
| Step 3: warning triangle 50 metres behind (before the bend if there is one) | Motor Vehicles (Driving) Regulations 2017, reg. 28: "on highways and major roads with fast speed, reflective traffic warning triangles shall be placed at a distance of fifty meters behind the broken-down vehicle"; "if there is a bend on the road where the vehicle is parked, reflective traffic warning triangles shall be placed before the bend." |
| Step 3: bonnet up signals a breakdown | Queensland: "if safe, pop your bonnet to show passing motorists that you have broken down." State Farm: "Pop the hood of your car to help alert other drivers." |
| Step 4: no signal on your own network, 112 can still connect through another operator's tower (only if some network is in range) | 112 India FAQ: "Even when the mobile operator network is not available, you can call the emergency number 112 with your mobile phone." (https://112.gov.in/faq). SOS Alarm (Sweden's 112 operator): "Normally, your call will take place via emergency roaming, which means that the phone calls through another operator." and the caveat "if no mobile operator has coverage, you cannot call 112." (https://www.sosalarm.se/en/our-important-mission/frequently-asked-questions-and-answers/for-private-individuals/112--good-to-know-before-you-call/technical-questions-about-calling-112/can-you-call-112-without-coverage/) |
| Wait off the road, away from traffic | Highway Code rule 275: "keep well away from your vehicle and moving traffic"; Queensland: "move behind a barrier or as far away from traffic as possible." |

Dropped: "walking 20 km takes about 4 hours" (could not open a walking-speed source this run).
