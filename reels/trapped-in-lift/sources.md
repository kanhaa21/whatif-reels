# Sources: What if you were trapped in a lift with no phone signal?

Checked 21 Nov 2026.

| Claim in the reel | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Don't pry or force the doors open; the lift might not be at a door opening | KONE, "Top 6 elevator myths debunked": "never pry an elevator's doors open either, because the elevator might not be in front of a door opening" (https://www.kone.com/global/en/newsroom/stories/top-6-elevator-myths-debunked.html). Otis: "do not try to force the doors open" (https://www.otis.com/en/us/w/5-steps-to-take-if-your-elevator-stops). NYC Buildings: "Never attempt to pry the elevator doors open" (https://www.nyc.gov/site/buildings/safety/elevator-safety.page). Technical Safety BC: "never try to force open doors" (https://blog.technicalsafetybc.ca/stuck-elevator-3-things-you-must-know?hsLang=en) |
| Step 1: press "door open"; sometimes the lift is at floor level | Otis: "push the door open button, as sometimes the elevator is at floor level and the door will open" |
| Step 2: press the alarm; the lift has its own alarm button and intercom | Otis: "use the elevator's emergency button, intercom or phone". KONE: "press the alarm button (or call for help on your phone), stay put, and wait to be rescued". Technical Safety BC: "Most elevators in BC have an emergency phone and bell". NYC Buildings: "Ring the alarm." |
| Step 3: you won't run out of air; lifts have air vents | Otis: "the elevator has air circulation openings, so there will be no limit on available air inside the cab". KONE lists running out of oxygen as a myth: "Elevator cars are well-ventilated", with international ventilation standards "so air moves freely in and out". Technical Safety BC: "It is equipped with ventilation" |
| Breathe slowly, don't panic | Technical Safety BC: "Take some long, deep breaths to calm the nervous system." KONE: "No matter what, don't panic." |
| Step 4: stay inside and wait; inside is the safest place | Technical Safety BC: "Staying inside the car is the safest place to be." Otis: "it is best for a "trapped" passenger to remain within the cab until trained personnel can remove them." |
| A stuck lift could start moving again at any time | Technical Safety BC: "A stuck elevator could start moving again at any time, so trying to exit the elevator on your own could lead to serious injury or even death." |
| The roof hatch is for rescuers, not for passengers | KONE: hatches "often exist for rescue purposes, but most can only be opened from the outside by trained rescue professionals", "Passengers should never attempt to use them as escape routes." Technical Safety BC: "Never try to escape through the hatches in the ceiling." |

Dropped: "the lift's alarm works without mobile signal" (lift emergency lines vary: some are landlines, some use their own SIM, so the reel only says the lift has its own alarm and intercom). Dropped: how long rescues take (Technical Safety BC says only that "in most cases the door will open in a few minutes"; no firm number). Dropped: calling 112 (the premise is no signal, and the sources disagree on when to call emergency services).
