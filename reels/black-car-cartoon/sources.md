# Sources: "Planning to buy a black car?" (3D cartoon reel)

Checked 21 Nov 2026.

| Claim | Source |
| --- | --- |
| In the sun, a black roof got up to 25 °C hotter than a silver one; the cabin was about 5 to 6 °C hotter | Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, "Cool Colors for Cars Could Improve Fuel Economy, Reduce Emissions" (17 Oct 2011): roof of the silver car "as much as 25°C (45°F) cooler than the roof on the black car"; cabin soak air "about 5 to 6°C (9 to 11°F)" cooler (https://heatisland.lbl.gov/news/11089/cool-colors-for-cars-could-improve-fuel-economy-reduce-emissions). Phys.org on the same study (https://phys.org/news/2011-10-silver-white-cars-cooler.amp) |
| Washing leaves tiny swirl marks; they exist on every colour but show far more on black | Detailed Image, Ask a Pro: "those same wash induced swirls exist on different colors, but it is more obvious on black" (https://www.detailedimage.com/Ask-a-Pro/seeing-issues-with-different-color-cars-what-you-need-to-know/); Jimbo's Detailing: on white or silver "the same scratches are often still there — you just can't see them as clearly" (https://jimbosdetailing.com/blogs/black-car-care/black-cars-swirls) |
| One big study found black cars (among other colours) had up to about 10 % higher crash risk than white in daylight | Monash University Accident Research Centre, Report 263 (Newstead and D'Elia, 2007), real police-reported crash data from two Australian states: "relative crash risks were higher for the colours listed compared to white by up to around 10%" in daylight (https://www.monash.edu/muarc/research/our-publications/muarc263); NRMA summary, which also notes driving behaviour matters far more (https://www.mynrma.com.au/open-road/advice-and-how-to/driving/car-colour-and-crash-risk) |

Not claimed: "black shows dust more" (Detailed Image says white shows general dirt more easily), dawn/dusk crash figures (not in the pages read), resale value.
