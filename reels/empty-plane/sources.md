# Sources: What if you woke up mid-flight and every seat was empty, including the cockpit?

Checked 2026-11-18. The empty plane is a made-up scenario. Each real-world claim the reel or its captions state, with where it was verified.

| Claim in the reel | Sources |
| --- | --- |
| 35,000 feet is a typical airliner cruising height (used as the scenario's altitude). | Military & Aerospace Electronics, "Why commercial aircraft cruise around 35,000 feet" ("there's a good chance the altitude will settle somewhere around 35,000 feet"; cruise heights vary, e.g. 33,000 to above 40,000). |
| If the autopilot is on, it holds height and direction by itself. | Mental Floss, "How does autopilot work on an airplane?" (altitude hold: "a desired altitude can be set and the aircraft will fly at that altitude"; heading: the airplane "will fly that heading"). Phrased with "if the autopilot is on". |
| Say Mayday three times. | Wikipedia, "Mayday" ("Convention requires the word be repeated three times in a row during the initial emergency declaration"). |
| 121.5 is the emergency channel that controllers listen to (captions: towers and planes listen). | Wikipedia, "Aircraft emergency frequency" (121.5 MHz is the civilian "International Aeronautical Emergency Frequency"; "monitored by most air traffic control towers" and "by many commercial aircraft"; in the UK by RAF Distress and Diversion cells). |
| Air traffic control talks you through it, step by step (on-screen: wings level, slow descent). | AOPA, "Passenger with no flying experience lands aircraft" (2022-05-12): the passenger "contacted air traffic control, and followed controllers' guidance to a safe landing"; the controller "talked the passenger through the basics", including keeping the wings level and starting a slow descent. |
| A passenger with zero flying experience once landed a small plane this way (safe landing). | AOPA (as above): Cessna 208 Caravan, Bahamas to Florida, landed at Palm Beach International, May 2022. No names used in the reel. |
| Many airliners can land themselves, but someone has to set it up. | Flight Deck Friend, "Can a passenger jet land automatically by itself?" (a passenger plane can land itself "through a system that is often referred to as 'autoland'"; "The pilots can program the autopilot to carry out the landing automatically" and supervise it; "The airport must have the radio navigation aid infrastructure installed to support an autoland"). |

Scenario, not claims: the black ocean, the storm, the alarms and the controller's "I can hear you." are dramatised parts of the made-up situation. The one joke is "Bhaiya, side mein roko!".

Revised 2026-11-18 for more suspense; no new factual claims were added.

Not stated on purpose: the 7700 emergency transponder code (source page could not be checked this run), and any claim that a passenger could set up autoland alone (not verified).
