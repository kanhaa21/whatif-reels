# Sources: What if you became invisible for a day?

- An invisible person would be blind. Vision needs the retina to absorb light; a fully invisible eye lets light pass through without being absorbed, so you cannot see:
  - qntm, "Invisibility theory": "a photon falls on your eyeball, your retina absorbs the photon, and a nerve impulse sends the image to your brain ... if you are invisible, then photons passing through your eyeball must pass through without being absorbed: thus, you cannot see." https://qntm.org/invisibility
  - Atlas Obscura, "The Problem With Invisibility Is The Blindness": "if a person achieved true, perfect invisibility, they would probably be blind." (quotes Stephane Larouche, Duke, on the reciprocity principle) https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/the-problem-with-invisibility-is-the-blindness
- Mosquitoes find people by breath and smell (and body heat as an added cue); their vision is poor: "sensors they've evolved to detect exhaled breath and odor from people", "Mosquitos' vision is notoriously poor" (HealthDay, 26 Aug 2024, on a Nature paper). https://www.healthday.com/health-news/infectious-disease/mosquitos-have-another-way-to-find-you-body-heat
- Dogs' sense of smell is "10,000 to 100,000 times as acute" as ours (PBS NOVA, citing James Walker and colleagues). The reel says "at least 10,000 times better", the low end of that range. https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/nova/article/dogs-sense-of-smell

Jokes and the hypothetical itself (waking up invisible, the boss, Delhi mosquitoes, gali dogs, free movie entry) are framed as the what-if, not as facts.
